Reflection 4

In the artist talk it was incredible meeting Sara Stern and seeing some of her projects and artworks. There was one film which I really liked, the film was about the love story of two disco ball people finding their other half. I really liked the effect in the beginning of the film where she made it look like the sand was alive and moving. It was creepy and eerie, the music also added on to that vibe. But I didn’t expect it to be a different type of story later on in the film. While watching the film I was curious how she was able to keep changing the pose for the stop motion animation without leaving footprints in the sand. After the film she showed us some behind the scenes and it was incredible to see the stop motion figure of the disco ball people were actually miniatures that were in a perspective that made them look life sized. Seeing these miniature figures helped answer my question. I also love how there wasn’t any voice over in the video but we still get the message and tone the film is trying to portray. I really liked the symbolism of the half of a disco ball finding each other to form a whole like finding someone out there that will make you complete. I also really like the half land and ocean shot in one of the scenes and the camera slowly gets submerged by the ocean wave. You can see how long these stop motion animation took based on the direction of the sun and its movement or the clouds in the back where some are slower than others and some are just incredibly fast. Another project I really like from Sara Stern is the one with the building and she used the lights inside the dark building to form a shape of eyes and mouth. The animated facial expressions make it seem like the building was a living thing. I think she used the third floor of the building as the eyes and the lower floor for the mouth. It’s also incredible how she perfectly timed each of the projectors to make them in sync with each other so that it doesn’t look wonky when played or notice any delays between each screen. Having this scene taken at night really makes the face pops out and become the focus point. I feel like if it was done during the day it wouldn’t be that eye-catching due to so many bright things in the scenery that the face of the building would probably blend in. It was really amazing seeing her work and how she made each of them.
